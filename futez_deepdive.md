Futez:
Core Concept: A Social Network for Football (Soccer) Fans
Futez is essentially the intersection of a live football scores tracker and a social review/discussion platform. Think of it as "Letterboxd or Goodreads, but for football matches." Here's how it works:
What users can do:

Rate matches — after a game, fans give it a score (e.g., 6.4, 7.2), rate sub-categories like Atmosphere, Quality, and Refereeing, and vote for Man of the Match (MOTM) or worst performer.
Rate individual players — each player in a match gets a crowd-sourced rating from fans, surfacing standouts and underperformers.
Write reviews — fans leave written reviews/opinions on games, similar to movie reviews, which others can upvote or downvote.
Follow other fans — there's a social graph (Following/Popular feed) so you can see what matches and reviews people you follow are engaging with.
Track matches across all leagues — the homepage is a date-based hub showing live, finished, and upcoming games from dozens of leagues worldwide (La Liga, Premier League, Bundesliga, Serie A, Copa del Rey, etc.), with engagement stats like number of viewers, comments, and ratings.
The key differentiator:
Rather than being just a scores app (like SofaScore or FlashScore), Futez adds a community layer — the aggregate fan rating becomes a meaningful signal about a match's entertainment value, and the platform turns watching football into a shared, expressive social activity.
It appears to be a Brazilian product (.com.br domain, Brazilian leagues like Gaúcho Championship included), but covers international football broadly.

Futez — Full Feature Breakdown
Home Page
The home is date-navigable (prev/next day or pick from a calendar). At the top sits a horizontal carousel of "featured matches" — the highest-engagement games of the day, each card showing team logos, score, a viewer count, a crowd-sourced rating ring, and a comment count. Below that is a Highlights of the Day carousel showing the top-rated individual players across all matches, with their position, number of votes received, number of views, and comment count.
The main match list below has two tabs: All (every match globally) and Your Matches (matches involving favorited teams). It can be filtered by four modes using pill buttons: Leagues (default, grouped by competition), Best (sorted by highest rating), Popular (sorted by viewer/engagement volume), and Commented (sorted by discussion activity). A status bar shows totals for Live, Finished, and Upcoming matches, and there's a search box to filter specifically by team or league name within that day's list.
Match Detail Page
This is the heart of the product. It contains:
Score card & context — Competition name, matchday/round, date/time, team logos and names, and live or final score.
Average Rating panel — A large number (e.g. 6.37 "DECENT") with a color-coded ring and a bar chart showing the distribution of ratings from 1–10. Crucially, you can swipe between three fan-perspective views: All Fans, Home Team Fans, and Away Team Fans — each showing a separate consensus rating and review count. This is a killer feature that surfaces how differently each fanbase perceived the same game.
Indicators — Three sub-ratings always shown alongside the overall rating: Atmosphere (stadium/crowd feel), Quality (football quality), and Refereeing (officiating quality).
MOTM & Underperformed cards — Surfaces the community's top-voted Man of the Match and the worst performer, with their player photo, score, and vote count/percentage.
All Players panel — A ranked list of every player from both squads (toggled by team tab), each with their crowd-sourced rating, number of votes, and number of comments. Players are ordered by rating. There is also a filter button to switch between the top 10, 50, etc.
Rate Match button — Opens a multi-step rating modal:

A slider from 1–10 for the overall match score (labeled, e.g. "DULL" at 5, presumably "DECENT", "GREAT", "EPIC" at higher values), plus a "No rating" option.
Three sub-sliders for Quality, Refereeing, and Atmosphere.
A "Rate Players" sub-screen listing every player from both squads with their own individual slider (1–10), an optional text comment per player, and MOTM / Worst buttons to nominate them specifically.
A final text comment box for the overall match review.
Favorite Match button — Saves the match to your favorites (used later in profile/notification tracking).
Share button — Shares the match page.
Match Discussion feed — A live comment thread where comments are tied to the scoreline moment they were written at (e.g. "14' | 1:0"). This is a clever way to give timeline context to comments.
Reviews section — Below the discussion sits a full paginated review list (separate from quick comments). It shows written reviews from other users, filtered by Following / Home Fans / Away Fans / Neutrals. Each review shows the reviewer's username, their national flag/badge, the streaming service they watched on (e.g. "TV · Dazn", "TV · ESPN+"), their overall match score, the review text, and upvote/downvote buttons. Sort options: Most Liked, Most Recent, etc. View toggle buttons let you switch between full-text view, score-only compact view, and a lineup/formation view.
Other Matches sidebar — Shows recent related matches (same teams, same competition) with their community rating, as a discovery rail.

Matches Page (Monthly View)
A separate monthly discovery view that shows: a "Most Watched" match carousel (the biggest match of the month by viewership), then a "Best and Worst" section that lets you filter by Avg Rating / Atmosphere / Quality / Refereeing to surface the objectively best and worst reviewed matches of the month across all leagues — globally, not just your favorites.
Feed
Two tabs: Following (reviews and ratings posted by people you follow) and Popular (trending reviews globally). Each card shows the match context at the top, then the review text, the reviewer's handle and streaming service badge, their match score, like/dislike counts, and comment count. This makes match reviews feel like social posts.
Search
A modal with three tabs: Teams, Leagues, and Users — requiring at least 3 characters to trigger results. This allows cross-entity discovery: find a club, follow a league, or find another fan to follow.
Notifications
A bell icon with a dropdown. The Settings page reveals granular push notification controls: toggles for Votes (someone likes/dislikes your review), Comments (replies to your reviews), New Followers, and Matches (reminders 30 min before and after matches you've marked as going to watch). You can also set up per-favorite-team notifications so you never miss a match alert.
Profile
A user page with four tabs:

Overview — Aggregate stats: total matches rated, average rating given, matches per day over time (bar chart), and a "Top Players" section showing which players the user has rated most highly.
Reviews — All of the user's written match reviews, sortable by Match Date, with ascending/descending order toggles and view mode options (text/score/lineup).
Players — All player votes cast by the user, filterable by: Best/Worst/MOTM/underperformed, Season (2024/2025/2026), Position (GK/DEF/MID/FWD/MGR), and Minimum Rating.
Teams/Matches Favorites — Tracked favorite teams and saved/favorited matches.
The profile header shows follower count and following count (both clickable), an Edit button, and a Share button.

Settings
Preferences section includes: a 2025 Wrapped feature (an annual recap of your year in football, Spotify-style), Language selection, and Notifications configuration. Support section links to Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, and Community Policy.
Product Plan: Tennez (a Futez for Tennis)
Tennis is a uniquely strong fit for this concept. Unlike football where you're rating a team performance, tennis fans are highly invested in individual players, specific rivalries, the drama of sets and tiebreaks, and the spectacle of Grand Slams vs. smaller tour events. Here's how to adapt every Futez feature for tennis.
Name & Concept
Tennez (or a similar name). The core idea: a social platform where tennis fans rate matches and players, discuss live during play, and build a community around shared love of the sport — match by match, point by point.
Home Page
The date-navigable home shows today's ATP, WTA, ATP Challenger, ITF, and Grand Slam matches in a carousel of featured cards. Each card shows: Player 1 vs. Player 2, set scores (e.g. 6–4, 3–6, 7–5), the tournament name and round, a community rating ring, and engagement counts (viewers, comments). A "Highlights of the Day" strip features the top-rated player performance of the day across all matches.
Filter modes for the match list would be: Tournaments (grouped by event), Best (highest rated), Electrifying (most engagement/hype), and Most Discussed.
Match Detail Page
Score card — Player names and flags, seed numbers, set-by-set scores, a service indicator showing who is serving. Surface type badge (Hard/Clay/Grass/Indoor).
Average Rating — Overall match quality score, with the now-familiar fan-perspective tabs: All Fans, Player 1 Fans, Player 2 Fans. The distribution chart and descriptive label (e.g. "THRILLING" for a high score).
Tennis-specific Indicators instead of Atmosphere/Quality/Refereeing:

Entertainment (how exciting/dramatic was it?)
Level of Play (quality of tennis, shot-making, consistency)
Umpiring/Chair Umpire (officiating quality, calls, time violations)
Bonus optional indicator: Crowd (stadium atmosphere — relevant for big events)
Player of the Match — In tennis this maps perfectly: one player per match gets a POTM vote (equivalent to MOTM), and there's an "Underperformed Expectations" vote for the player who disappointed relative to their ranking/form.
Set-by-set breakdown — A unique tennis feature: community sentiment per set. After the match, fans can indicate which set was the best, showing how a 3-setter was experienced beyond just the final score.
Rate Match modal — Overall slider + Entertainment/Level/Umpiring sub-sliders + Rate Players section where you rate both players' performance individually (not just the winner) + optional text review.
Match Discussion — Live comment thread with score context stamps (e.g. "3rd Set, 5-5" context tags on comments), plus a post-match tab. Fan perspective filter: All / Player A fans / Player B fans / Neutrals.
"Watching On" badge — Users note which streaming service they watched on (Tennis Channel, Amazon Prime, beIN Sports, etc.), adding social context to reviews just like Futez does.
Favorite Match and Share buttons identical to Futez.

Matches Page (Monthly View)
Monthly view shows: Most Watched match of the month, Best-Rated by Entertainment / Level of Play / Umpiring, and crucially a Biggest Upsets section (where a lower-ranked player beat a much higher-ranked one, flagged by the community). Also a "Best Rivalries" section that surfaces the most-discussed head-to-head matchups of the month.
Feed
Following and Popular tabs identical to Futez. The Popular feed emphasizes Grand Slam matches, major upsets, and the biggest rivalries — Djokovic vs. Alcaraz, Swiatek vs. Sabalenka type content naturally rises to the top. The "Watching On" streaming badge on reviews helps fans discover where to watch upcoming matches.
Search
Teams → Players (search for Federer, Sinner, Gauff, etc.)
Leagues → Tournaments (search for Roland Garros, Wimbledon, Indian Wells, etc.)
Users → Users (same as Futez)
Notifications
Same architecture as Futez but tennis-specific:

Votes — someone liked your review
Comments — replies to your reviews
New Followers
Matches — 30-minute pre-match reminder and post-match rating prompt for favorited players or tournaments
Favorites — Per-favorite-player alerts (your favorite player is on court now)
Upsets — Optional "big upset alert" when a top-10 player loses to someone ranked 50+

Profile
Same four-tab structure:

Overview — Total matches rated, average rating given, matches per day chart, Top Players rated (your highest-rated performances across all players)
Reviews — All written match reviews with sorting
Players — All player votes cast, filterable by Season, Tour (ATP/WTA/Both), Surface (Hard/Clay/Grass), Tournament Tier (Grand Slam/Masters/250), and Minimum Rating
Tournaments — Saved favorite tournaments and favorited individual matches
A tennis-specific addition: Surface Stats — a visual breakdown of your rating behavior by surface (do you rate clay court matches higher? Are you a Wimbledon-obsessive?).

Settings
Same structure as Futez: Year Wrapped (your year in tennis stats — matches watched, favorite players, most dramatic match of the year, surface distribution), Language, Notifications, and a Community Policy.
Tennis-Specific Differentiators vs. Futez
The most exciting things tennis unlocks that football doesn't:
The Rivalry Engine. Tennis has a finite number of top players and recurring rivalries. The platform can surface "this is the 14th time these players have met — here's how fans rated their 5 most recent encounters" as a match preview feature, building narrative continuity across seasons.
Surface identity. Fans feel deeply about clay vs. grass specialists. Filtering reviews and ratings by surface creates an entirely different reading of a player's career.
Grand Slam tournament mode. During a Grand Slam (Wimbledon, Roland Garros, US Open, Australian Open), the platform activates a special tournament bracket view — fans rate each round's matches and the community consensus bracket emerges showing which matches were the best of the tournament.
Pre-match "Watch Intent." Before a match, users can mark "I'm watching this" (Futez has this for notifications). In tennis, this creates a real-time viewer count that makes even smaller Challenger matches feel like events when 500 fans say they're watching.
Serve and return stat overlays. When match data is available, show fans the official stats (aces, first-serve %, winners) alongside the community rating, creating a bridge between data-driven and emotional fan experiences.
